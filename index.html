Daily Macro Analysis

The economy is not running on one switch. Politics is pricing war-risk and export rules at the same time: Mideast news still puts a premium on oil, freight, and gold, while chip-export controls keep splitting the supply chain into scarce product and China-exposed product. Rates sit over all of it — a Fed cut can loosen money for quality firms and memory, but only if the cut is disinflation, not a broken labor market; if oil or grain lifts living costs, that cut is delayed and growth stocks pay first. Resources refuse to move as a bloc: oil and gas follow supply and conflict, copper follows factories and power, rare earths follow policy, grain follows the food bill. On today’s tape that already shows up as a split market — energy, gold, and hard-to-make chips hold up; airlines, travel, leveraged growth, and one-day themes give way. Tomorrow do not buy the index as one idea. Keep cash and gold if conflict or oil is leading; add chips, memory, or materials only after a pullback and only in the lane that actually printed; do not stack DRAM with Micron, and do not treat China talk, copper, or an AI headline as the same trade.
